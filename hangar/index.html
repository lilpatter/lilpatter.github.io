About

Switches the signed-in Steam account without the account picker or a password prompt, shows every game on every account, and brings your Steam friends, chat and profiles with it. Steam stays in the tray, holding a fraction of its usual memory in RAM.

Why Hangar Exists

Steam keeps one account signed in at a time. Switching means opening its account picker and watching Steam restart in front of you, typing a password and a Steam Guard code for any account it has forgotten, and the library only shows the games of whoever is signed in. If the game you want is on your other account, you find out after the switch.

Hangar makes switching one click, shows every game on every account in one library, and switches for you when you press Play on a game another account owns. It does it with the sign-ins Steam already remembers, so it never needs your password.

Features

  • Silent switching, straight to the tray
  • Play as… launches a game on the account that owns it
  • Your whole library, installed or not
  • Friends, chat and profiles, signed in with a QR code
  • Idle Steam holds 30–70 MB in RAM instead of about 330 MB
  • Never reads or stores passwords

Under the Hood

  • Less RAM without breaking SteamLaunch flags barely help: the best set saved about 25 MB of Steam's ~330 MB, because most of it is a hidden web renderer every client keeps loaded. Instead, Hangar asks Windows to trim the working set of steam.exe and steamwebhelper (EmptyWorkingSet), a minute after Steam signs in and every five minutes after, never while a game is running. The working set is the part of a program's memory held in RAM. Idle in the tray, Steam's drops from about 330 MB to 30–70 MB, and it stays signed in. Steam's total allocated memory doesn't shrink: the trimmed pages wait in standby or the page file and come back if Steam touches them. Measured on Windows 11 with Steam signed in, idle in the tray and no game running.
  • No passwords, everAccounts connect with a QR code from the Steam Mobile App, so Hangar never sees a password. The sign-in token it gets back is stored encrypted with Windows DPAPI, readable only by your Windows account.
  • Its own line to SteamFriends, chat and profiles run over Hangar's own connection to Steam's servers, so the Steam client stays signed in alongside it and games still launch through Steam as usual.

Privacy & Security

What it connects to

  • Steam: QR sign-in, your library, friends, chat, profiles and game artwork
  • Steam Web API: only if you add your own key, to list games an account owns but hasn't played on this PC
  • GitHub: to check for and download updates

What stays on your PC

  • Hangar never sees, reads or stores your Steam password
  • The sign-in token Steam gives Hangar is encrypted with Windows' own data protection (DPAPI), so only your Windows account can read it
  • Settings and favourites
  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reports, and nothing is ever sent to PG.

Downloads and updates

  • Updates itself from its GitHub Releases page and checks each download against its SHA-256 hash before installing. You can turn this off under Settings › About.
  • The downloads aren’t code-signed yet, so Windows may say the publisher is unknown. To check a download is the real one, compare its SHA-256 hash with the ones shown here.
