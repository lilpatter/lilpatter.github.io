About

Finds the latest Game Ready and Studio drivers for your NVIDIA card, checks they're signed by NVIDIA and installs them with one prompt, without the NVIDIA App.

NVIDIA's own way to keep a GeForce driver current is the NVIDIA App, which installs its own overlay, background services and update checks alongside the driver. Most people only want the driver.

Verdant does that one job: it finds the right driver for your exact card, checks it really comes from NVIDIA, installs it with one prompt and keeps the NVIDIA App out.

Screenshots

Screenshots from Windows 11. The Mac-style sidebar and window buttons are part of Verdant’s design; it runs on Windows only.

Features

  • Matches your exact card with NVIDIA's catalog
  • Only runs installers signed by NVIDIA
  • Never installs the NVIDIA App
  • Express or custom install, with roll back
  • Live GPU temperature, load and VRAM
  • Compact mode: a small window that keeps you current

Under the Hood

  • Keeping the NVIDIA App outVerdant unpacks NVIDIA's driver package itself and runs the installer from what's left. The NVIDIA App's folders are never extracted, any component the package marks as part of it is removed, and the install is refused if one remains. If NVIDIA's installer adds the app anyway, Verdant uninstalls it.
  • Only NVIDIA's own installersBefore anything runs, Verdant checks the driver installer is signed by NVIDIA and refuses it if not, so a tampered download never gets a chance to run.

Privacy & Security

What it connects to

  • NVIDIA: your card's model, to find matching drivers, their release notes and the driver downloads
  • Steam store: artwork for the games each driver is made for
  • GitHub: to check for and download updates

What stays on your PC

  • Your GPU, driver version and live readings are read on your PC
  • Drivers download to Downloads\NVIDIA Drivers
  • Only installers signed by NVIDIA Corporation are run
  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reports, and nothing is ever sent to PG.

Downloads and updates

  • Updates itself from its GitHub Releases page and checks each download against its SHA-256 hash before installing. You can turn this off under Settings › About.
  • The downloads aren’t code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say “Windows protected your PC” and that the publisher is unknown. Choose More info, then Run anyway. To check a download is the real one, compare its SHA-256 hash with the ones shown here.
