About

Gives every game its own vibrance, contrast and white balance, switched on when the game takes focus and off when you leave it. Works on any graphics card.

Why Prism Exists

Colour settings in a graphics driver are global. Turn vibrance up for one game and the desktop, every video and every other game change with it, so you end up flipping settings by hand each time you switch.

Prism gives each game its own colours and puts the desktop back the moment you leave the game. It began as Digital Vibrance, rewritten in Rust so it works the same on any graphics card and sits in the tray using almost nothing.

Features

  • Switches with the game in focus, or while it runs
  • Vibrance up to 200%, past the driver
  • Contrast, brightness and white balance too
  • NVIDIA, AMD and Intel, no admin rights
  • Nothing injected into your games
  • About 4 MB of memory in the tray

Under the Hood

  • Idle means idleOne engine thread owns the colour effect. It sleeps until Windows reports a new window in front, and only checks again while a profile needs it. Closing Prism's window destroys it and its web view entirely, so in the tray it uses about 4 MB and no CPU. That's its private memory as well as its working set (the part held in RAM), measured on Windows 11 with Prism idle in the tray.
  • Better than the originalPrism is a rewrite of Digital Vibrance in Rust. Along the way: the live preview no longer sticks on screen, a colour change Windows refuses is retried, and the list of running processes is no longer read every 1.5 seconds.

Credits

  • Digital Vibrance by SYTStudio Prism is a rewrite of Digital Vibrance in Rust, and imports its profiles.

Privacy & Security

What it connects to

  • GitHub: to check for and download updates. Prism connects to nothing else

What stays on your PC

  • Your game profiles and settings
  • Prism watches which window is in front to switch profiles. That never leaves your PC
  • Nothing is injected into your games, and no admin rights are needed
  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reports, and nothing is ever sent to PG.

Downloads and updates

  • Updates itself from its GitHub Releases page, also while it's in the tray, and checks each download against its SHA-256 hash before installing. You can turn this off under Settings › About.
  • The downloads aren’t code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say “Windows protected your PC” and that the publisher is unknown. Choose More info, then Run anyway. To check a download is the real one, compare its SHA-256 hash with the ones shown here.

Requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit
  • Any NVIDIA, AMD or Intel graphics card
  • Games in borderless or windowed mode: exclusive fullscreen skips the effect, and HDR may change it
  • The Microsoft Edge WebView2 runtime, which comes with Windows 11
